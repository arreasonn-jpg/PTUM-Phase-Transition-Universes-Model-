The aim of the present paper is therefore broader than
a phenomenological dark-radiation study. We pursue three
linked goals:
(i) to formulate PTUM as a cosmological creation sce-
nario based on a discharge event, establishing the the-
oretical framework and its effective field theory;
(ii) to show that the resulting effective dark-sector struc-
ture is internally consistent within the tested param-
eter regime, with perturbation stability, EFT valid-
ity, and compatibility with BBN and isocurvature con-
straints;
(iii) to test the observable consequences of the model
against current cosmological data through a Bayesian
benchmark analysis, profile likelihood, and dataset-
split diagnostics.
Scope and claim boundaries. Throughout this work,
PTUM is treated as an effective cosmological frame-
work, not as a completed first-principles theory of the
origin of the Universe. The model does not derive the
metastable pre-discharge background, does not solve the
cosmological-constant hierarchy by itself, and does not
specify a UV completion. Its present claims are narrower:
it defines a threshold-triggered phase-transition scenario,
implements the associated proxy-fluid perturbations, and
identifies an observational discriminator between fluid and
free-streaming dark radiation.
To this end, we proceed in two steps. First, we con-
struct the effective field theory of PTUM, derive its equa-
tions of motion, study the primordial creation sector—
including exact-background inflationary dynamics—and
identify the parameter region in which the late-time dark-
energy sector remains observationally viable. Second, we
implement the proxy-fluid perturbations in the CLASS
Boltzmann code [12,13], compute the cosmological signa-
tures, and constrain the model using a Bayesian bench-
mark analysis with Cobaya [14]. The resulting constraints
are cross-checked through profile likelihood extraction and
dataset-split analysis, and confronted with BBN, isocur-
vature, and linear stability requirements.
We find that PTUM is observationally viable: the pfd
abundance is bounded to ∆Nfluid < 0.31 at 95% credi-
bility from the full data combination, model comparison
gives ∆AIC = 2.26 relative to ΛCDM, and all consistency
checks are satisfied. The Hubble constant shifts upward
by ∼0.8 km s−1 Mpc−1 relative to ΛCDM, partially alle-

2 Conceptual overview of PTUM
PTUM begins with a multiverse-like background contain-
ing a scalar quantum fuel field Φ. The central idea is that
local accumulation of this field drives a threshold-triggered
discharge process. Once a critical configuration is reached,
the effective mass of a proxy activation mode becomes
negative, generating a tachyonic transition that activates
string-like degrees of freedom.
The basic sequence is:
(a) Fuel accumulation. The scalar fuel field Φ accumu-
lates in a metastable environment. The effective poten-
tial is chosen such that it is approximately quadratic
at small field values but saturates to a plateau at large
field values.
