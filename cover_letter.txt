Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled “Phase Transition Universes Model: A Discharge-Based Cosmological Creation Scenario with a Unified Dark Sector” for consideration in your journal.

The manuscript develops the Phase Transition Universes Model (PTUM), an effective-field-theory cosmological scenario in which a finite-energy discharge event sources the hot expanding Universe and generates a unified dark sector. The framework connects an early fuel-field sector, a discharge-triggered transition, a superheavy dark-matter candidate sector, a residual dark-energy component, and a proxy-fluid dark-radiation sector within a single dynamical construction.

The central observational signature is the proxy-fluid dark-radiation component. Unlike free-streaming dark radiation, this component is modeled as a coherent effective fluid with equation of state \(w=1/3\), sound speed \(c_s^2=1/3\), and vanishing anisotropic stress within the effective linear proxy-fluid description. Consequently, it does not produce the neutrino-induced CMB acoustic phase shift associated with free-streaming relativistic species. This distinction gives the model a clear falsifiable signature.

The manuscript includes an implementation of the proxy-fluid sector in CLASS/Cobaya and compares the model with Planck 2018, BAO, CMB lensing, and Pantheon+ data. The resulting bound \(\Delta N_{\rm fluid}<0.31\) at 95% credibility shows that the model remains observationally viable, while current data still mildly prefer \(\Lambda\)CDM under information criteria. The paper therefore presents PTUM not as a replacement for \(\Lambda\)CDM already favored by current data, but as a testable discharge-based extension with a distinctive future CMB signature.

The manuscript also discusses theoretical consistency and limitations, including EFT validity, perturbative stability, the need for a more fundamental derivation of the metastable pre-discharge background, the absence of a full reheating-sector model, and the open question of UV completion. These limitations are presented explicitly to make the model’s domain of validity and falsifiability clear.

I believe the paper will be of interest to readers working on early-Universe cosmology, dark radiation, CMB phenomenology, effective field theory, and models of the dark sector.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. I have reviewed and approved the final version and take full responsibility for its content.
